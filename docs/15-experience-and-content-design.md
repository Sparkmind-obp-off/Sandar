# 15 — Experience and Content Design

## Experience principles
- Calm, respectful, non-judgmental language.
- A user can start with a task, not a life-story questionnaire.
- Ask one useful question at a time.
- Make assumptions and uncertainty visible.
- Prefer practical next steps over motivational slogans.
- Never use shame, fear, countdown pressure, or streak mechanics.

## Information architecture
- Home: explain SANDAR and offer task entry points.
- Plan: private workspace and user-owned goals.
- Financial Relief: cash-flow overview and scenario tool.
- Resource Navigator: source-backed public listings.
- Opportunities: user-selected learning/work listings (later phase).
- Settings & Privacy: data, consent, export, deletion, accessibility.

## First-screen content
The first screen should explain:
1. What SANDAR helps with.
2. That it does not know the visitor's private situation.
3. That personal information is optional and user-controlled.
4. That SANDAR is not an emergency, legal, medical, lending, or investment service.

## Interaction patterns
- Progressive disclosure; do not show every module at once.
- Explain why a field is requested.
- Support “I don't know”, “not sure”, “skip”, and ranges where practical.
- Preview before save.
- Undo or edit user-entered values.
- Confirm destructive actions and clearly explain their scope.
- Provide a plain-language source link for public information.

## Content rules
Avoid labels such as poor, failed, irresponsible, vulnerable, unstable, or at-risk. Prefer neutral descriptions such as “amount not yet covered”, “date to review”, “information not confirmed”, and “option to explore”.

## Accessibility
Target WCAG 2.2 AA as a design goal: semantic structure, keyboard access, visible focus, sufficient contrast, form labels, error summaries, reduced motion, and screen-reader announcements.
